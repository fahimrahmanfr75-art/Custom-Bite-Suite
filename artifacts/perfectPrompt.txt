Creating a prompt that makes an AI fully understand your intent—and exceed your expectations—requires moving from a simple question-and-answer model to a "structured instruction" approach. High-quality prompting acts as a roadmap, reducing the AI's need to guess.

Here is how to create a "master" prompt, incorporating techniques from the search results.

1. The Anatomy of a Perfect Prompt (The CODE Framework)
To get the best results, structure your prompt using the CODE formula: Context, Own script, Documents, and Elements:
Context: Tell the AI why you are doing this, who it is acting as (role), and the target audience.
Own Script (Instruction): Use clear action verbs like "Write," "Summarize," or "Analyze."
Documents/Data: Attach relevant files, text, or data for the AI to analyze.
Elements (Constraints & Format): Define the length, tone, style, and structure (e.g., Markdown table, JSON).

2. Techniques to Make the AI "Think" Better
Instead of just asking for an output, guide the AI's reasoning process:
Role-Playing ("You are a..."): Start your prompt by assigning a persona, such as "You are a senior UX designer with 20 years of experience".
Chain-of-Thought ("Think Step-by-Step"): Ask the AI to explain its reasoning before giving the final answer. This catches errors and results in a more logical, comprehensive output.
Few-Shot Prompting (Show, Don't Just Tell): Provide 1–3 examples of the desired input-output format within your prompt. This helps the AI mimic your desired tone and structure perfectly.

3. How to Make the AI "Better Than Expected"
To get creative, superior results, you must invite the AI to think beyond the immediate prompt:
Reverse Prompting ("Before you start, ask me questions"): End your prompt with: "Before you start, ask me any questions you need to ensure the output is perfect".
Explicit Creative Permission ("Hallucinate Creative Solutions"): For brainstorming, tell the AI: "This is a creative session. Think out-of-the-box and provide 3 conventional ideas and 2 unconventional, breakthrough ideas".
Self-Consistency (Generate & Select): Ask the AI to generate three different versions of the output, then ask it to select the best one based on your constraints.

4. Example: Before vs. After
Weak Prompt: "Write a marketing email for a new water bottle."
Master Prompt (Using Persona, Context, Constraints, & Goal): "You are an expert direct-response copywriter. Write a 100-word marketing email for a new water bottle (The Hydro-Titan).
Context: The bottle is indestructible and keeps water cold for 48 hours. The target audience is busy urban professionals.
Constraints: Use a professional yet punchy tone. Avoid cliché phrases like 'unleash your potential.'
Goal: Get them to click the link to purchase.
Format: Include a catchy subject line and 3 bullet points of benefits.
Before writing, ask me any clarifying questions about the product to ensure success."

5. Final Step: Iterate
Rarely is the first prompt perfect. If the output is not quite right, do not just rephrase it. Tell the AI what was wrong: "That output was too formal. Make it more conversational, and focus more on the 'indestructible' aspect".
